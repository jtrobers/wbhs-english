Long Way Down
Floors 3 & 2

Students examine Frick and Shawn while considering perspective, pressure, grief, and whether the Rules still make sense.

Formative

Activity Overview

In this reading activity, students continue the elevator investigation by examining Frick on Floor 3 and Shawn on Floor 2.

Students use the Elevator Case File organizer to analyze how perspective complicates Will's assumptions and how Shawn's presence increases the emotional pressure of Will's decision.

Learning Targets

  • I can identify key details about Frick and Shawn.
  • I can analyze how a new perspective changes the meaning of an event.
  • I can explain how pressure affects Will's thinking and choices.
  • I can use one strong quote to support an interpretation.
  • I can evaluate whether the Rules still seem reliable by this point in the novel.

Materials

  • Long Way Down novel
  • Elevator Case File organizer: Floors 3 & 2
  • Pencil or pen
  • Canvas assignment post, if used

SOL Correlations

These correlations apply only to this activity: reading Floors 3 and 2, analyzing Frick and Shawn, evaluating perspective, and completing an evidence-based response.

11.RL.1.C — Characterization Through Events and Dialogue

Students examine how Frick and Shawn are revealed through what they say, what they do, and how Will reacts to them.

11.RL.2.B — Author's Craft and Style

Students analyze how Reynolds uses pacing, silence, structure, and dialogue to build emotional tension near the end of the elevator ride.

11.RL.2.C — Literary Devices and Meaning

Students consider how the elevator, repeated floor structure, and ghostly encounters develop conflict, symbolism, and theme.

11.RV.1.F — Figurative Language and Nuance

Students interpret language connected to pressure, grief, memory, and the idea of being haunted by the past.

11.W.1.B.ii — Analytical Writing

Students use evidence to explain how Frick or Shawn changes Will's thinking about revenge and the Rules.

11.C.1.B — Building on Others' Ideas

Students participate in discussion by comparing interpretations, asking questions, and responding to classmates' claims with evidence.

Student Directions

  1. Review the question: How does seeing another person's perspective change the choices we make?
  2. Read Floors 3 and 2 of Long Way Down.
  3. Complete the Elevator Case File organizer for Frick and Shawn.
  4. For each character, choose one important quote and explain why it matters.
  5. Track how Frick adds another side of the story and how Shawn adds pressure to Will's decision.
  6. Complete the final reflection about whether Will should follow the Rules.

Teacher Notes

Purpose

This activity should help students see that the elevator ride is no longer just about Will's grief. Frick complicates the story by adding another perspective, while Shawn forces Will to confront the emotional weight of the Rules and the choice he is about to make.

Suggested Flow

  • Bell ringer: Can learning another person's side of the story change what someone believes?
  • Read Floor 3 and complete the Frick side of the case file.
  • Discuss how Frick changes the way readers understand the chain of violence.
  • Read Floor 2 and complete the Shawn side of the case file.
  • Discuss the pressure Will feels after seeing Shawn.
  • Final reflection: After meeting Shawn, should Will follow the Rules?

Discussion Starters

  • What perspective does Frick add to the story?
  • Why is it important that Frick is more than just a name connected to violence?
  • How does Frick challenge Will's assumptions?
  • What role does Shawn play in Will's life?
  • Why does Shawn's appearance create more pressure instead of giving Will an easy answer?
  • At this point, do the Rules seem strong, broken, or dangerous?

Exit Ticket

After meeting Frick and Shawn, should Will follow the Rules? Answer in a short paragraph using one specific detail or quote from Floors 3 and 2.

Resources

Use the Elevator Case File organizer while reading Floors 3 and 2. The organizer will help students collect evidence, examine perspective, and prepare for Will's final decision.
