Long Way Down
First Floor & Final Decision

Students evaluate the final question, interpret the ambiguous ending, and determine what lessons Will has learned throughout the elevator ride.

Formative

Activity Overview

This concluding activity asks students to synthesize evidence from the entire novel and complete the Final Elevator Report.

Students revisit each ghost, evaluate the Rules, and develop their own interpretation of the ending. Rather than searching for one correct answer, students support their thinking with evidence and reasoning.

Learning Targets

  • I can explain the lesson each ghost might teach Will.
  • I can support a claim using evidence from multiple parts of the novel.
  • I can analyze the significance of the ending.
  • I can evaluate whether Will should follow the Rules.
  • I can explain how an author's choices contribute to meaning.

Materials

  • Long Way Down novel
  • Final Elevator Report
  • Completed Elevator Case Files
  • Pencil or pen
  • Canvas assignment post, if used

SOL Correlations

These correlations apply only to this activity: reading the ending, synthesizing the case files, making a claim, supporting an interpretation, and explaining Reynolds' author choices.

11.RL.1.A — Theme Development

Students analyze how themes of grief, revenge, cycles of violence, and choice develop throughout the novel and culminate in the ending.

11.RL.1.B — Evidence-Based Interpretation

Students use evidence from multiple sections of the novel to support an interpretation of Will's final decision and the novel's message.

11.RL.2.B — Author's Craft and Style

Students evaluate how Reynolds uses structure, pacing, silence, repetition, and ambiguity to shape reader interpretation.

11.RL.2.C — Literary Devices and Meaning

Students consider how the elevator structure, repeated encounters, and final open-ended moment contribute to meaning.

11.W.1.B.i — Claims and Evidence

Students develop a claim about what Will should do or what the ending suggests and support it with relevant evidence.

11.W.1.B.ii — Reasoning

Students explain how their evidence supports their claim and connects to the larger theme of choices and consequences.

Student Directions

  1. Review your completed Elevator Case Files.
  2. Read Floor 1 and the ending of Long Way Down.
  3. Complete Page 1 of the Final Elevator Report by identifying one lesson Will might learn from each ghost.
  4. Complete Page 2 by answering the final response questions about the Rules, the open elevator door, and the ambiguous ending.
  5. Use evidence from throughout the novel to support your responses.
  6. Prepare to discuss your interpretation of the ending.

Teacher Notes

Purpose

This lesson serves as the culmination of the elevator investigation. Students should move beyond comprehension and begin evaluating ideas, interpretations, and author choices. The goal is not to determine one correct ending but to support an interpretation with evidence.

Suggested Flow

  • Bell ringer: What advice would you give Will before the elevator reaches Floor 1?
  • Review the six case file characters and the lessons each might teach Will.
  • Read Floor 1 and the ending of the novel.
  • Discuss the final line: "You coming?"
  • Complete the Final Elevator Report.
  • Pair discussion: Compare interpretations of what Will does next.
  • Whole-class debrief: Why might Reynolds leave the ending unresolved?

Discussion Starters

  • What lesson does Buck teach Will?
  • Which ghost has the greatest impact on Will?
  • Should Will follow the Rules?
  • What do you think happens after the elevator door opens?
  • Why doesn't Reynolds tell us exactly what Will does?
  • Why might ambiguity be more powerful than a clear answer?

Exit Ticket

What is the most important lesson Will learns during the elevator ride? Use evidence from at least two ghosts to support your answer.

Resources

Use the Final Elevator Report to organize evidence, evaluate the Rules, and develop an interpretation of the ending.
